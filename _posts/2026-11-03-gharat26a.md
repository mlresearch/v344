title: 'Memories That Discriminate: Detecting and Correcting Bias in Personalized
  Hiring Agents'
abstract: Memory modules in LLM-based agents enable continuity across interactions
  with users, especially to deliver personalized experiences. While agents constantly
  interact with memory, their interpretation of memory contents is crucial to how
  agents might behave next. Thus, any one-sided interpretation (stereotypical or otherwise)
  of some memory content by an agent might lead to unintended biases in its actions
  which the LLM guardrails might fail to catch (especially when explicit stereotypical
  phrases are not present). Moreover, while bias and safety issues in LLMs have been
  extensively studied, similar studies are largely absent in memory-enhanced LLM-based
  agents. Thus, considering the use case of a hiring agent, in this paper, we configure
  and test a hiring agent orchestrating different types of workflows. We seek to understand
  whether and when agents are prone to one-sided interpretations of memory content,
  and if it can happen, what would be the impact and how to prevent it. Our experiments
  reveal that one-sided memory misinterpretations can happen based on the context
  and potential fields (e.g., gender, race, or other attributes of interest), and
  as a result bias is introduced and propagated through an agent workflow (even when
  the LLM used is already safety-trained). This leads to bias and prejudice by agents
  even when it is not intended or preferred by the users, emphasizing the need for
  additional protective measures. Our proposed Fairness Regulation Learning (FRL)
  and Task-Aware In-Context Fairness Regulation Learning (TA-ICFRL) reduce such one-sided
  interpretations by injecting fairness-aware or balancing instructions during task
  execution.
